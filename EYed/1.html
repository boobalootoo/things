This guide is a simplified summary for parents. It combines guidance from Development Matters, Birth to 5 Matters, and the Statutory EYFS Framework.

Statutory Health Checks

In the UK, all children have regular health and development reviews (Healthy Child Programme). These are the big ones to look out for.

The 2 to 2½ Year Review

Usually carried out by a Health Visitor or Nursery Nurse. It may happen at your home, a GP surgery, or a children's centre.

What they look for:

  • General Development: Movement, speech, social skills.
  • Growth: Height and weight check.
  • Teeth: Advice on brushing and dentist visits.
  • Behavior: Sleeping, eating, and potty training support.

Tip: If your child attends nursery, the key person will also do a "Progress Check at Age 2" which feeds into this.

School Entry Review (Age 4-5)

Usually happens when your child starts Reception class. It assesses if they are ready for school learning.

Key Areas:

  • Vision & Hearing: Detecting any issues early.
  • Growth: Measuring height and weight (National Child Measurement Programme).
  • Vaccinations: Checking 4-in-1 pre-school booster.

When to ask for help

Children develop at different rates, but you should speak to a GP or Health Visitor if you notice these signs.

By 18 Months

  • Not walking independently.
  • Not saying any single words.
  • Does not point to show you things.

By 2 Years

  • Does not put two words together (e.g., "More milk").
  • Does not run.
  • Doesn't copy actions or words.

By 3 Years

  • Speech is unclear to strangers.
  • Falls over a lot or finds stairs difficult.
  • Doesn't play make-believe.

General Red Flags

  • Regression: Losing skills they once had.
  • Eye Contact: Avoiding eye contact.
  • Hearing: Not responding to their name.

Who to contact?

Your local GP, Health Visitor, or the SENCO at your child's nursery/preschool are the best first steps.

Jargon Buster

About These Documents

In England, early years providers follow specific frameworks. It can be confusing for parents, so here is the breakdown:

EYFS (Statutory Framework)

The Law. It sets the standards for safety, ratios, and what children must achieve by age 5.

Development Matters

Guidance on how children learn. It's not a tick-list, but a guide to help professionals support your child.

Birth to 5 Matters

Alternative guidance created by the Early Years Coalition. It focuses heavily on the unique child and positive relationships.
